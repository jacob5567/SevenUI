# SevenUI in detail

Everything SevenUI changes, part by part, with its settings and how to build and test it. The [README](https://github.com/jacob5567/SevenUI/blob/main/README.md) has the short version.

## What it changes

- **Damage numbers:** the floating damage numbers use JetBrains Mono, a monospace font, in place of the game's pixel digits, with a thinner version of the game's grey outline and dark edge, and its damage colors. They keep the font's narrower shape and are spaced to match.
- **Flat HUD:** the game turns the HUD's corners slightly inward, so they lean toward the middle of the screen. SevenUI levels them so the whole HUD faces the screen squarely.
- **Text:** HUD text uses Barlow instead of the game's Bombardier font, and numbers (the run timer, your level, item and buff stacks, and skill and equipment cooldowns), the dollar sign and the boss's name use Barlow Condensed. Barlow's lines are centered on its capitals, so text centered in a box or beside an icon isn't drawn low. This covers new HUD text too: item and buff icons, notifications, ally cards, the scoreboard, and panels added by other mods. Void Fiend's corruption percentage keeps the game's font.
- **Run info:** the timer, the run's difficulty and the stage and level share one flat translucent panel, wider than the game's. The timer is at the left; at the right are the difficulty's name in the accent color, in place of its icon (an Eclipse run's level as "Eclipse 3"), and the stage and monster level on one line under it. The scrolling difficulty segments are replaced by a thin bar that fills as the current segment progresses, with the segment's name beside it in spaced capitals. While Karma is on, a second row under it shows eight segments filled up to the run's karma, with "KARMA 4" beside them in blue, in place of the game's Karma icon. While any artifact is on, a row under those shows the artifacts' icons, with "ARTIFACTS" at its right, in place of the game's artifact panel under the run info; the icons keep the game's tooltips, and shrink to share the row's one line once more than seven are on. The timer reads minutes and seconds, "24:51", without the hundredths, and the panel is the design's height, with the panels under it a small gap below.
- **Health and level:** laid out as on SevenUI's design. The level is at the left, a large accent number under a small "LV" header. Beside it are the health numbers ("1,240 / 1,520", the current health in bold) with the health regeneration ("+12.4/s") at the right, the health bar under them, and a thin accent experience bar under that. The bars are flat on a dark trough: health green and shield blue, with the health red once it is under 25%, when the numbers turn pink too. The barrier is a pale gold strip along the top of the health bar, so the health and shield under it still show, maximum health a curse has taken away is striped dark, and the health one-shot protection covers (while under a tenth of your health is missing) is striped green, under the barrier and the other effects drawn over the health. Bars whose pattern means something else, such as the execute threshold, keep the game's look.
- **Scoreboard and chat:** the scoreboard (Tab) is a dark flat board, 1220 pixels wide at 1080p, near the top of the screen between its left edge and the run info (`ScoreboardBesideRunInfo`). Each player has the portrait on a flat square, the name in bold over the survivor and level and the item count ("40 items · 22 9 4 2 1 2", the total then each tier's count in its color), the key item, the equipment with its orange outline, and the money in gold. Their items sit in flat slots, sorted by tier (common, uncommon, legendary, boss, lunar, void, then any other), in the order they were picked up within each tier. `ScoreboardLayout` picks how players are shown:
  - **Rows** (the default): a row per player, split by thin lines, your own tinted. Each row has the portrait, the name block with the money at the right of the name, the key item slot, the equipment, and then the items, wrapping under themselves, the row growing a line at a time. Pointing at an item outlines it in every row that holds it and dims the rest.
  - **Tabs:** a list of the players down the board's left, each line with the portrait, the name block with the money at the right of the name, and smaller key item and equipment slots. The shown player's line is tinted with an accent line down its left, and their items fill the middle of the board. Pointing at a player's line, or selecting anything in it with a gamepad, shows that player, and the board opens on your own. The list is narrower than a row, so a long name or a long item count line can be cut short.

  In the rows layout the items are 40 pixels; in the tabs layout they are 52, 48 or 44 pixels while they fit two lines, and 40 after. The item you point at (or select with a gamepad) is described in a panel docked in a column at the board's right, instead of the tooltip that would cover the items; the board grows to hold it if the players are shorter. It shows the item and its name in its tier's color, its full description (or LookingGlass's description and stats when LookingGlass is installed), and every player who holds it, with their stack. It stays on the last item until you point at another. If the board would run past the bottom of the scoreboard's panel, the slots shrink, down to 24 pixels. On a gamepad the focused part still lights up, as a thin outline. The chat's input field, shown while you type, is outlined in thin grey on a faint dark face, with a thin accent strip down its left edge.
- **Tooltips:** tooltips of things in the HUD (items, skills, equipment) are laid out as on SevenUI's design: a dark panel with a thin strip of the item's tier color along its top (orange for equipment), a header with the name in bold and an item's stack count ("×3") at the right, then the description. LookingGlass's item stats sit apart under a thin line, a row per stat with its name in grey and its value in the accent color at the right. The blue outline is gone. Tooltips in menus are left as they are.
- **Item pickers:** the panels that ask you to pick an item (Command's cubes, the Void, Cursed and other potentials, the Lemurian egg, the cursed book and the like) are flat. Up to six options sit side by side as cards, each with the item centered on a dark band across its top, the tier's color along the band's foot, and its name and description under it, the cards wide enough to keep LookingGlass's "With this item, you will have:" on one line; more options sit in a grid of large dark slots, as many to a row as the game puts there, with the hovered item's name and description under it (the first item's until you hover one). As in the item bar, a slot shows how many of the item you have once you have two or more. The hovered option gets a thin accent outline, and Cancel is a small outlined button under the panel, thin and grey until you hover it. Panels with layouts of their own, such as the scrapper's list and the drone scrapper, keep the game's look.
- **Pause menu:** "PAUSED" in large condensed capitals, with a line of the run under it (the time, the stage and level, and the difficulty in the accent color, as the run info shows them), over one centered stack of outlined buttons like the pickers' Cancel: a faint dark face and a thin grey outline, lit with an accent outline while hovered. Quit To Menu and Quit To Desktop sit side by side in a smaller row under the rest. The game's blurred backdrop, the lobby panel and the menus the buttons open (settings, the logbook) keep their look.
- **Prompts and notifications:** the interaction prompt sits on a flat translucent panel at the HUD's right edge, with a strip along its top in the color of the cost (gold for money, red for health, blue for lunar coins, the tier's color for items, and the accent when it is free); a cost in lunar coins shows the same flat coin as the top left. Each row is the key on a small grey keycap, then the action ("Open chest") and its cost in bold without brackets; the Inspect prompt, when shown, is a second row, its text lined up with the first. Pickup notifications are smaller and higher than the game's, as on the design: a flat translucent panel with a strip along its top in the item's tier color (a drone's in its kind's color, a Winnowing trial's in green), the item's icon on a dark slot, and its name in bold white over its description in grey. Drone and Winnowing trial notifications look the same, their icons on the same slot without the game's frames behind them. When an item turns into another, as when a void item corrupts one, the slot is wider and holds the old item and the new one at the same size, with a thin arrow between them and a strip along the slot's foot, both shading from the old item's tier color to the new one's.
- **Ally cards and buffs:** ally cards sit on flat panels with flat health bars like yours. Allies of one kind (the same name, following the same player) share one card, so a crowd of drones doesn't cover the chat: "Gunner Drone ×8", with its bar split into a segment per drone, each showing that drone's health in green, or red under a quarter. A kind of one keeps its own card and the game's bar, with its shield and barrier. Players always have their own cards. Each buff sits on a dark square like an item, with a strip along the bottom: green for a buff, red for a debuff, gold for a cooldown. Buffs sit in rows above the level and bars, wrapping onto another row above once they would run past the bars' width. A buff's stack count is a bold Barlow Condensed number in its square's top-right corner, shown once it has more than one stack, and LookingGlass's buff timers sit under the squares.
- **Skills and equipment:** the slots are flat and dark, at the design's size and spacing, and the equipment and key item slots are the same size as the skills, with thin dividers between the key item and the skills and between the skills and the equipment. A recharging skill gets a dark cover over the top of its icon that shrinks as it recharges, in place of the game's radial sweep, and the game's white ready outline is gone (the icon still turns grey while a skill isn't ready). The skills' and the equipment's charge counts sit inside their slots' bottom-right corners. The equipment keeps a thin orange ready outline, and the key item gets a thin blue one in the game's key item color while it is ready. Cooldowns and stocks use Barlow Condensed, and the key labels (including the inventory and sprint keys) are small grey capitals without their dark plates. The scoreboard (Tab) and sprint buttons' icons are flat white strokes: a short list of rows for the scoreboard and a double chevron for sprint, pointing down while you fly a drone by remote operation, when sprint descends. The game's tutorial popups over these buttons (equipment, sprinting and remote operation) are dark panels with an accent strip along the top and white text, in place of yellow boxes with black text. Drone Tech's drones and Seeker's lotus, which the game hangs above the skills, keep their place over the larger row and grow with it.
- **Top:** money and lunar coins sit side by side, each on its own flat panel fitted to its amount, without the cluster's frame. Money is gold and lunar coins pale blue, with a flat coin, a thin ring around a crescent, in place of the game's shaded one. The item bar loses its frame and, like the boss bar under it, takes the middle 47% of the screen's width, so a full row stays clear of the run info. Each item sits on a dark slot with its tier's color along the bottom, and stack counts are Barlow Condensed numbers instead of sprite digits. The boss bar is flat in a muted red. Above it, one row holds the boss's name in Barlow Condensed capitals, its subtitle and its health ("2,730 / 2,730"). Health the boss just lost trails the fill in dark red and catches up as quickly as in the game.
- **Panels:** the panels under the run info (objectives, and panels other mods add there) are flat and translucent, with a small gap between them. The objective panel's title sits in a header strip in spaced capitals, and each objective is left-aligned after a small flat square outline, which becomes the game's check or cross once the objective is done or failed. The teleporter's charge shows its percentage at the right in the accent color, with a thin bar under the objective that fills as it charges. The Winnowing trials that track their progress with a bar (Trial of Infernal Greed and Trial of Causality) get the same thin bar in place of the game's bar and the badge beside their text. Objectives are drawn without the game's inline icons (the teleporter's icon after "Teleporter"), so "Find and activate the Teleporter" fits on one line.

Not yet restyled: the buff icons themselves, and menus outside the HUD other than the pause menu.

## LookingGlass

[LookingGlass](https://thunderstore.io/package/DropPod/LookingGlass/) works alongside SevenUI. Its stats panel, buff timers and item counters are added to the game's own HUD objects, so they pick up the restyle like the rest of the HUD. Its item stats go in their own section of the tooltip, under the description. When its `Full Description in Hud` setting is on, the item pickers show each option's full description with LookingGlass's stats under it, as its own picker tooltips do ("With another stack, you will have:" above them in italics); otherwise they show the game's short pickup text. The stats panel is a copy of the objective panel, so it gets the same flat panel and a header strip of its own ("STATS" and "LOOKINGGLASS"), with the stats in two columns under it, each name in grey with its value in bold white at its right. LookingGlass's colors for the values aren't kept, and a line of a customized stats string that isn't "Name: value" shows as a name alone.

## Multiplayer

The mod only changes how your own HUD looks. It never changes gameplay and never sends anything over the network, so it works when you join a host who doesn't have it installed.

## Configuration

Edit `BepInEx/config/sevenstack.SevenUI.cfg`, or use the in-game settings menu if [Risk Of Options](https://thunderstore.io/package/Rune580/Risk_Of_Options/) is installed (optional).

| Setting | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `BarlowFont` | true | Draw HUD text in Barlow instead of the game's font. Languages the game draws in Noto Sans keep it. |
| `MonospaceDamageNumbers` | true | Draw the floating damage numbers in JetBrains Mono, a monospace font, instead of the game's pixel digits. |
| `FlatPanels` | true | Draw the HUD flat: facing the screen squarely instead of leaning in, with flat translucent panels, flat bars and slots, and the health numbers as text. Off brings back the game's look. Everything under "What it changes" except the fonts. |
| `TightMargins` | true | Draw the HUD's corners near the screen's edges, as on SevenUI's design: 2.6% of the screen's height in (2.2% at the top), and the ally cards 2.6% in and 28% down. Off keeps the game's margins, 5% of the screen. Only with `FlatPanels` on. |
| `ScoreboardBesideRunInfo` | true | Place the scoreboard as on SevenUI's design: near the top of the screen, centered between its left edge and the run info. Off centers it on the screen, where the game puts it. Only with `FlatPanels` on. |
| `ScoreboardLayout` | Rows | How the scoreboard shows items. Rows: a row per player, with their items after their equipment, grouped by tier. Tabs: one player's items at a time, picked from a list of the players at the left. Either way, the item you point at is described in a panel on the scoreboard, with who holds it. Only with `FlatPanels` on. |
| `DebugLogging` | false | Log extra detail, and enable the dump key. |
| `DebugAlwaysShow` | false | Show the mod's visuals even when they wouldn't normally appear, for testing. |
| `DumpKey` | F8 | With `DebugLogging` on, press to log each HUD's fonts and what the mod restyled. |

## Notes

- Barlow has Latin letters only. Languages the game draws in Noto Sans (Russian, Chinese, Japanese, Korean and others) keep the game's font. Letters Barlow lacks in other languages are drawn in the game's font.
- Barlow is baked into a font atlas when the first HUD appears, which takes a moment once per game launch.

## Building

Requires the .NET 8 SDK, a local Risk of Rain 2 install, and [Risk Of Options](https://thunderstore.io/package/Rune580/Risk_Of_Options/) in the r2modman profile (it is only referenced for the settings menu). `dotnet build -c Release` builds the plugin and copies it into the r2modman Default profile; add `-p:CopyToProfile=false` to skip the copy. `./build.sh` also writes the Thunderstore zip to `dist/`. The default game and profile paths are in `Directory.Build.props`; override them on the command line or in a `Directory.Build.props.user` file.

`tests/` holds in-game tests, run with [ModTestRunner](https://github.com/jacob5567/ModTestRunner). ModTestRunner builds them into a separate dev-only plugin, which is never packaged: clone it and run `./build-tests.sh <path to this repo>` there. Then start a run with DebugToolkit installed, and enter `mod_test --mod SevenUI` in the console, which runs every check and takes every screenshot. `mod_test --mod SevenUI --suite screenshots` runs only the screenshots: it sets up each restyled part of the HUD (items, the scoreboard, shield and barrier, cooldowns, the N'tormat's pages beside the key item, the interaction prompt, a Lunar Pod's prompt, the tutorial popups, a pickup notification, the Winnowing's trial objectives, ally cards, damage numbers, damage numbers from Lost Seer's Lenses' instakills, the boss bar, and the HUD of each survivor with elements of its own: Railgunner, Void Fiend, Seeker, Drone Tech, Drifter and Acolyte) and saves a screenshot of each in SevenUI's look and the game's, in `BepInEx/ModTestRunner/Screenshots/`. The screenshots turn the player into each survivor and back, and start the teleporter, last in their suite; each suite starts on a fresh stage as the survivor the run began with. `mod_test --mod SevenUI --suite scoreboard` runs the scoreboard's checks and its screenshots, with a spread of items and with 3 to 100 different items: the items' grid in each layout, switching the layout with the scoreboard open and turning the flat look off after, the docked panel describing the item pointed at in each layout, and the item bar's tooltip over the open scoreboard. `mod_test --mod SevenUI --suite pickers` opens every item picker in the game (Command for each group of items and equipment, and for an item it offers alone, and every other picker panel, opened from a Command cube), checks its layout and screenshots it in both looks, and checks the descriptions with LookingGlass's setting off and on. `mod_test --mod SevenUI --test <name>` runs one scenario, such as `boss_bar`; `mod_test --mod SevenUI list` names every test by suite. Survivors and the N'tormat are skipped without their expansion.
