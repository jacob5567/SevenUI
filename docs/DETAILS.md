# SevenUI in detail

Everything SevenUI changes, part by part, with its settings and how to build and test it. The [README](https://github.com/jacob5567/SevenUI/blob/main/README.md) has the short version.

## What it changes

- **Damage numbers:** the floating damage numbers use JetBrains Mono, a monospace font, in place of the game's pixel digits, with a thinner version of the game's grey outline and dark edge, and its damage colors. They keep the font's narrower shape and are spaced to match.
- **Flat HUD:** the game turns the HUD's corners slightly inward, so they lean toward the middle of the screen. SevenUI levels them so the whole HUD faces the screen squarely.
- **Text:** HUD text uses Barlow instead of the game's Bombardier font, and numbers (the run timer, your level, item and buff stacks, and skill and equipment cooldowns), the dollar sign and the boss's name use Barlow Condensed. Barlow's lines are centered on its capitals, so text centered in a box or beside an icon isn't drawn low. This covers new HUD text too: item and buff icons, notifications, ally cards, the scoreboard, and panels added by other mods. Void Fiend's corruption percentage keeps the game's font.
- **Run info:** the timer, the run's difficulty and the stage and level share one flat translucent panel, wider than the game's. The timer is at the left; at the right are the difficulty's name in the accent color, in place of its icon (an Eclipse run's level as "Eclipse 3"), and the stage and monster level on one line under it. The scrolling difficulty segments are replaced by a thin bar that fills as the current segment progresses, with the segment's name beside it in spaced capitals. While Karma is on, a second row under it shows eight segments filled up to the run's karma, with "KARMA 4" beside them in blue, in place of the game's Karma icon. While any artifact is on, a row under those shows the artifacts' icons, with "ARTIFACTS" at its right, in place of the game's artifact panel under the run info; the icons keep the game's tooltips, and shrink to share the row's one line once more than seven are on. The timer reads minutes and seconds, "24:51", without the hundredths, and the panel is the design's height, with the panels under it a small gap below.
- **Health and level:** laid out as on SevenUI's design. The level is at the left, a large accent number under a small "LV" header. Beside it are the health numbers ("1,240 / 1,520", the current health in bold) with the health regeneration ("+12.4/s") at the right, the health bar under them, and a thin accent experience bar under that. The bars are flat on a dark trough: health green and shield blue, with the health red once it is under 25%, when the numbers turn pink too. The barrier is a pale gold strip along the top of the health bar, so the health and shield under it still show, maximum health a curse has taken away is striped dark, and the health one-shot protection covers (while under a tenth of your health is missing) is striped green, under the barrier and the other effects drawn over the health. Bars whose pattern means something else, such as the execute threshold, keep the game's look.
- **Scoreboard and chat:** the scoreboard (Tab) is a dark flat board, 1220 pixels wide at 1080p, near the top of the screen between its left edge and the run info (`ScoreboardBesideRunInfo`). Each player has the portrait on a flat square, the name in bold over the survivor and level and the item count ("40 items · 22 9 4 2 1 2", the total then each tier's count in its color), the key item, the equipment with its orange outline, and the money in gold. Their items sit in flat slots, sorted by tier (common, uncommon, legendary, boss, lunar, void, then any other), in the order they were picked up within each tier.
  - **Rows:** a row per player, split by thin lines, your own tinted. Each row has the portrait, the name block with the money at the right of the name, the key item slot, the equipment, and then the items, wrapping under themselves, the row growing a line at a time. Pointing at an item outlines it in every row that holds it and dims the rest.
  - **Tabs:** with more than one player, once the rows' items would need to be smaller than 32 pixels to fit, the rows collapse to their top lines (the portrait, the name block, the key item and the equipment) and only one player's items show, after the equipment, running down from that player's row (or up from the board's bottom when they would run past it). That player's row and items are drawn as one tab: a tinted shape with a solid outline, the items centered across it a full line at a time, at least 16 pixels in from its edges. Pointing at a player's row, or selecting anything in it with a gamepad, shows that player, and the board opens on your own. With `ScoreboardTabs` off the rows never collapse, and the items shrink to fit instead.

  The items are 40 pixels. The item, equipment or key item you point at (or select with a gamepad) is described in a panel docked in a column at the board's right, instead of the tooltip that would cover the items; the board grows to hold it if the players are shorter. It shows the icon and the name in its color (an item's tier, the equipment's orange, or the game's color for lunar and boss equipment and key items), the full description (or LookingGlass's description and stats when LookingGlass is installed), and, once the run has more than one player, every player who holds it, with their stack of an item (alone, you see no list, since your stack is on your icon). It stays on the last one until you point at another. If the board would run past the bottom of the scoreboard's panel, the slots shrink: with more than one player down to 32 pixels, before the rows collapse, and for a player alone down to 24 pixels. On a gamepad the focused part still lights up, as a thin outline. The game's inspect panel under the board (what Tab shows for the pickup, chest, shrine or drone next to you) is restyled in place, as on the design: it spans the board's width, 6 pixels under it, on the board's flat face, with a 2 pixel strip along its top, the icon on a dark 56 pixel slot, and the name in bold over the description in the body color. The strip and the name are the color the game gives the thing (an item's tier, a drone's kind, a chest's own color), and the description keeps the game's own text colors; the lore line is hidden. With LookingGlass's `Full Description in Hud` on, an item's stats leave the description for a column at the panel's right: after the name and description a 14 pixel gap, a 1 pixel line the height of the content, another 14 pixels, then a 200 pixel column with a row per stat, its name in grey at the left and its value in bold in the accent color at the right (at least 16 pixels apart); an item without stats, an interactable, or the setting off leaves the panel as it was. With `FlatPanels` off the game's panel comes back. The chat's input field, shown while you type, is outlined in thin grey on a faint dark face, with a thin accent strip down its left edge.
- **Tooltips:** tooltips of things in the HUD (items, skills, equipment) are laid out as on SevenUI's design: a dark panel with a thin strip of the item's tier color along its top (orange for equipment), a header with the name in bold and an item's stack count ("×3") at the right, then the description. LookingGlass's item stats sit apart under a thin line, a row per stat with its name in grey and its value in the accent color at the right. The blue outline is gone. Tooltips in menus are left as they are.
- **Item pickers:** the panels that ask you to pick an item (Command's cubes, the Void, Cursed and other potentials, the Lemurian egg, the cursed book and the like) are flat. Up to six options sit side by side as cards, each with the item centered on a dark band across its top, the tier's color along the band's foot, and its name and description under it, the cards wide enough to keep LookingGlass's "With this item, you will have:" on one line; more options sit in a grid of large dark slots, as many to a row as the game puts there, with the hovered item's name and description under it (the first item's until you hover one). As in the item bar, a slot shows how many of the item you have once you have two or more. The hovered option gets a thin accent outline, and Cancel is a small outlined button under the panel, thin and grey until you hover it. A Lemurian egg used with no items to give opens the same flat panel with its header and Cancel and a short grey "No items to offer" in place of the grid (the game shows an empty panel). Panels with layouts of their own, such as the scrapper's list and the drone scrapper, keep the game's look.
- **Countdowns:** the panels the game puts under the top of the screen for a timed event (the moon's escape, a Winnowing trial, Champ Rush's doom timer) are flat, with the event's name in spaced capitals on a header strip, the time left as `02:05` in Barlow Condensed, and a thin accent bar under it that drains with the time. In the last 10 seconds the bar turns red and the time a lighter red; a trial's own warning colors are kept for its time. The bar's full length is the countdown's whole duration when the game's controller for it is found (the escape's, the doom timer's), and otherwise the longest time the panel has shown. The panel sits under the item bar and the boss bar, and moves with them. The infinite tower's timer keeps the game's look.
- **Pause menu:** "PAUSED" in large condensed capitals, with a line of the run under it (the time, the stage and level, and the difficulty in the accent color, as the run info shows them), over one centered stack of outlined buttons like the pickers' Cancel: a faint dark face and a thin grey outline, lit with an accent outline while hovered. Quit To Menu and Quit To Desktop sit side by side in a smaller row under the rest. The game's blurred backdrop, the lobby panel and the menus the buttons open (settings, the logbook) keep their look.
- **Dialog boxes:** the game's confirmation and message dialogs (quit confirmation, a settings change's "keep these?" prompt, and the menus' messages) get a flat dark panel, Barlow text, and the pause menu's buttons: a faint dark face and a thin grey outline, lit with an accent outline while hovered or selected with a gamepad. `DialogBoxStyler` on the plugin watches the open dialogs, so it works in menus too. Turning `FlatPanels` off puts the game's look back on dialogs already open; one opened while it is off keeps the game's look.
- **Prompts and notifications:** the interaction prompt sits on a flat translucent panel at the HUD's right edge, with a strip along its top in the color of the cost (gold for money, red for health, blue for lunar coins, the tier's color for items, and the accent when it is free); a cost in lunar coins shows the same flat coin as the top left. Each row is the key on a small grey keycap, then the action ("Open chest") and its cost in bold without brackets; the Inspect prompt, when shown, is a second row, its text lined up with the first. Pickup notifications are smaller and higher than the game's, as on the design: a flat translucent panel with a strip along its top in the item's tier color (a drone's in its kind's color, a Winnowing trial's in green), the item's icon on a dark slot, and its name in bold white over its description in grey. Drone and Winnowing trial notifications look the same, their icons on the same slot without the game's frames behind them. When an item turns into another, as when a void item corrupts one, the slot is wider and holds the old item and the new one at the same size, with a thin arrow between them and a strip along the slot's foot, both shading from the old item's tier color to the new one's.
- **Ally cards and buffs:** ally cards sit on flat panels with flat health bars like yours, and stay clear of the chat. Allies of one kind (the same name, following the same player or ally) share one card: "Gunner Drone ×8", with its bar split into a segment per drone, each showing that drone's health in green, or red under a quarter. A kind of one keeps its own card and the game's bar, with its shield and barrier, and other players always have their own cards, which stay at full size and are never folded. The cards keep the game's order, in sections: each player's card followed by that player's allies, then your own allies last. While every card fits above the chat's messages at the game's size, each shows that way. Once the next card would reach the chat, the cards of single allies become 28-pixel rows (a small portrait, then the name and the health bar side by side), while shared cards keep their size and segments. If that is still too tall, each section keeps the allies' cards that fit and folds the rest into a row of its own under them: a portrait for each kind it holds (up to five) and "+3 more", counting the allies on those cards. Room goes out in priority order, though the sections stay in the order above: the player cards first, then a row for every section that has allies, then your own allies, then the other players' allies from the top down, each leaving room for the rows of the sections below it. If the player cards and those rows alone don't fit, every ally goes under its section's row, the player cards run on over the chat and your own row comes after them. The allies in that row have no health bars shown. As allies leave, the cards come back the same way. While the scoreboard is open, the ally cards are hidden, so they don't draw over its left edge. Each buff sits on a dark square like an item, with a strip along the bottom: green for a buff, red for a debuff, gold for a cooldown. Buffs sit in rows above the level and bars, wrapping onto another row above once they would run past the bars' width. A buff's stack count is a bold Barlow Condensed number in its square's top-right corner, shown once it has more than one stack, and LookingGlass's buff timers sit under the squares.
- **Skills and equipment:** the slots are flat and dark, at the design's size and spacing, and the equipment and key item slots are the same size as the skills, with thin dividers between the key item and the skills and between the skills and the equipment. A recharging skill gets a dark cover over the top of its icon that shrinks as it recharges, in place of the game's radial sweep, and the game's white ready outline is gone (the icon still turns grey while a skill isn't ready). The skills' and the equipment's charge counts sit inside their slots' bottom-right corners. Bandit's Desperado stacks show as the buff's icon and a number, side by side and centered above the special slot while Desperado is equipped and there is at least one stack (Lights Out hides it), for whoever you are watching. The equipment keeps a thin orange ready outline, and the key item gets a thin blue one in the game's key item color while it is ready. The equipment you can switch to (with Functional Couplers, or MUL-T's second) is a deck of icons above the equipment slot, in place of the game's slot beside it, which the tight margins would put off the screen: the next one right-aligned over the slot, greyed with its seconds while it recharges, with a small chevron pointing down into the slot and, with a Coupler, your Interact and Equipment keys to its left ("E + Q"; MUL-T switches with Retool instead), and up to two more rising behind it, each fainter, in the order the switch reaches them. When you switch, the next icon grows down into the slot, the deck moves down a place, and the equipment you were using shrinks into the back, in a quarter of a second. Cooldowns and stocks use Barlow Condensed, and the key labels (including the inventory and sprint keys) are small grey capitals without their dark plates. The scoreboard (Tab) and sprint buttons' icons are flat white strokes: a short list of rows for the scoreboard and a double chevron for sprint, pointing down while you fly a drone by remote operation, when sprint descends. The game's tutorial popups over these buttons (equipment, sprinting and remote operation) are dark panels with an accent strip along the top and white text, in place of yellow boxes with black text. Operator's drones and Seeker's lotus, which the game hangs above the skills, keep their place over the larger row and grow with it; Operator's display is then shrunk back to the size the game draws it at, keeping its bottom right corner over the row. Drifter's display of what it carries, hung from the same place, is shrunk back the same way. Its junk meter sits beside the crosshair, and SevenUI leaves it alone.
- **Top:** money and lunar coins sit side by side, each on its own flat panel fitted to its amount, without the cluster's frame. Money is gold and lunar coins pale blue, with a flat coin, a thin ring around a crescent, in place of the game's shaded one. The item bar loses its frame and, like the boss bar under it, takes the middle 47% of the screen's width, so a full row stays clear of the run info. Each item sits on a dark slot with its tier's color along the bottom, and stack counts are Barlow Condensed numbers instead of sprite digits. The boss bar is flat in a muted red. Above it, one row holds the boss's name in Barlow Condensed capitals, its subtitle and its health ("2,730 / 2,730"). The game draws one fill for the boss's health, shield and barrier together, with one trail behind it for all three; SevenUI draws the boss bar as the player's health bar is, in its colors and in the fill's scale (the group's health, shield and barrier over its largest observed maximum): health the boss just lost trails it in dark red and catches up as the player bar's trail does, while a hit to the shield or barrier leaves no trail; health just healed shows in a lighter red until the trail catches up; a blue shield after the health (pink for void shields, as in the game, such as Plasma Shrimp's); a pale gold strip along the top for the barrier, from the bar's left end (the barrier's share at the end of the game's fill is covered, so the barrier adds no length); Lunar Ruin over the health, in the player bar's own style; dark stripes at the end of the bar for maximum health a curse took away; and a thin line where the boss would be executed: at 30% of its health while it is frozen, or, for an elite boss, at the Old Guillotine threshold of the player the HUD shows (a teammate while spectating), whichever is higher. With several bosses, each part is summed over them all. The threshold pips stay as in the game. With the flat panels off, the game's bar is back and the parts are gone.
- **Panels:** the panels under the run info (objectives, and panels other mods add there) are flat and translucent, with a small gap between them. The objective panel's title sits in a header strip in spaced capitals, and each objective is left-aligned after a small flat square outline, which becomes the game's check or cross once the objective is done or failed. The teleporter's charge shows its percentage at the right in the accent color, with a thin bar under the objective that fills as it charges. The Winnowing trials that track their progress with a bar (Trial of Infernal Greed and Trial of Causality) get the same thin bar in place of the game's bar and the badge beside their text. Objectives are drawn without the game's inline icons (the teleporter's icon after "Teleporter"), so "Find and activate the Teleporter" fits on one line. With MusicDisplay installed, the last panel in the column is "Now playing": a header strip with "NOW PLAYING" in spaced capitals, then the track's name in the text color and under it its author (if there is one) in the header grey, each on one line with an ellipsis if it is too long, in the same size and padding as the objectives. It sits below every other panel in the column, whatever comes and goes above it, and takes no space while there is no track. SevenUI reads the track from MusicDisplay's text, which it turns off (only its rendering; its place, size and alignment are not changed), so the track shows whether or not MusicDisplay's text has faded. With `FlatPanels` off there is no panel and MusicDisplay's text is as the mod has it.

Not yet restyled: the buff icons themselves, and menus outside the HUD other than the pause menu and dialog boxes.

## LookingGlass

[LookingGlass](https://thunderstore.io/package/DropPod/LookingGlass/) works alongside SevenUI. Its stats panel, buff timers and item counters are added to the game's own HUD objects, so they pick up the restyle like the rest of the HUD. Its item stats go in their own section of the tooltip, under the description. In the scoreboard's inspect panel they go in a column at its right (with `Full Description in Hud` on; the panel takes LookingGlass's own text from the panel or from the tooltip the pointer is on). When its `Full Description in Hud` setting is on, the item pickers show each option's full description with LookingGlass's stats under it, as its own picker tooltips do ("With another stack, you will have:" above them in italics); otherwise they show the game's short pickup text. The stats panel is a copy of the objective panel, so it gets the same flat panel and a header strip of its own ("STATS" and "LOOKINGGLASS"), with the stats in two columns under it, each name in grey with its value in bold white at its right. LookingGlass's colors for the values aren't kept, and a line of a customized stats string that isn't "Name: value" shows as a name alone.

## Multiplayer

The mod only changes how your own HUD looks. It never changes gameplay and never sends anything over the network, so it works when you join a host who doesn't have it installed.

## Configuration

Edit `BepInEx/config/sevenstack.SevenUI.cfg`, or use the in-game settings menu if [Risk Of Options](https://thunderstore.io/package/Rune580/Risk_Of_Options/) is installed (optional).

| Setting | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `BarlowFont` | true | Draw HUD text in Barlow instead of the game's font. Languages the game draws in Noto Sans keep it. |
| `MonospaceDamageNumbers` | true | Draw the floating damage numbers in JetBrains Mono, a monospace font, instead of the game's pixel digits. |
| `FlatPanels` | true | Draw the HUD flat: facing the screen squarely instead of leaning in, with flat translucent panels, flat bars and slots, and the health numbers as text. Off brings back the game's look. Everything under "What it changes" except the fonts. |
| `TightMargins` | true | Draw the HUD's corners near the screen's edges, as on SevenUI's design: 2.6% of the screen's height in (2.2% at the top), and the ally cards 2.6% in and 28% down. Off keeps the game's margins, 5% of the screen. Only with `FlatPanels` on. |
| `ScoreboardBesideRunInfo` | true | Place the scoreboard as on SevenUI's design: near the top of the screen, centered between its left edge and the run info. Off centers it on the screen, where the game puts it. Only with `FlatPanels` on. |
| `EquipmentDeck` | true | Show the next equipment as a deck of icons above the equipment slot with a Functional Coupler. Off shows no deck, only the slot. Only with `FlatPanels` on. |
| `RestyleItemPickers` | true | Restyle the panels where you pick an item, such as Command and Void Potentials, as cards or a grid in the flat look. Off keeps the game's look for them, open now or opened later. Only with `FlatPanels` on. |
| `CompactAllyCards` | true | While the ally cards would reach down over the chat, make the cards of single allies compact rows, and fold the cards that still don't fit into a "+N more" row under each player's allies. Other players' cards are exempt from both, and each player's allies fold into a "+N more" row of their own. As in the game, an ally's card is indented when the ally it follows has a card: so another player's allies, and that player's row, sit indented under the player, while the viewer's own allies don't, as the viewer has no card. Off keeps every card at full size and never folds any, so the cards may overlap the chat. Only with `FlatPanels` on. |
| `GroupAllyCards` | true | Allies of one kind (the same name, following the same leader) share one card, with "×N" after the name and a health segment per ally in place of the bar. Off shows one card per ally, as the game has it, with its own name and health bar. Only with `FlatPanels` on. |
| `LevelHud` | true | On faces the whole HUD squarely, undoing the game's turn of the corner clusters about the vertical axis (about 6 degrees, leaning in under the HUD camera's perspective). Off keeps that inward lean of the corners, with everything else flat; the skill row and the margins are measured through the camera either way. Only with `FlatPanels` on. |
| `ScoreboardDetailPanel` | true | On describes the item, equipment or key item pointed at in the scoreboard in `ScoreboardDetailPane`, a panel docked at the board's right that the board's width keeps room for, and hides the game's tooltip. Off shows the game's tooltip over the pointed-at item instead, and the board keeps no room for the panel. Only with `FlatPanels` on. |
| `ScoreboardTabs` | true | On collapses the scoreboard's rows to tabs when there is more than one player and the items would need to be smaller than 32 pixels to fit. Off never collapses them: every row keeps its items, shrunk to fit. Only with `FlatPanels` on. |
| `RestylePauseMenu` | true | Restyle the pause menu: the title, the run's line and the outlined buttons. Off keeps the game's look for it, open now or opened later. Only with `FlatPanels` on. |
| `RestyleTooltips` | true | Restyle the tooltips of items, equipment and skills in the HUD: a dark panel, a strip of the tier color, a header band and the stack count. Off keeps the game's look for them, with the font still following `BarlowFont`. Read as each tooltip opens, so a tooltip already open keeps its look. Only with `FlatPanels` on. |
| `TimerHundredths` | false | On shows the run timer's hundredths, as in the game, which the restyled run info leaves out; the rest of the run info stays restyled. Off shows the timer as `24:51`. Takes effect at once. Only with `FlatPanels` on. |
| `ShowHealthRegen` | true | Off hides the health regeneration text, such as `+12.4/s`, beside the player's health numbers; the game's HUD has no such text. Takes effect at once. Only with `FlatPanels` on. |
| `DebugLogging` | false | Log extra detail, and enable the dump key. |
| `DumpKey` | F8 | With `DebugLogging` on, press to log each HUD's fonts and what the mod restyled. |

## Notes

- Barlow has Latin letters only. Languages the game draws in Noto Sans (Russian, Chinese, Japanese, Korean and others) keep the game's font. Letters Barlow lacks in other languages are drawn in the game's font.
- Barlow is baked into a font atlas when the first HUD appears, which takes a moment once per game launch.

## Building

Requires the .NET 8 SDK, a local Risk of Rain 2 install, and [Risk Of Options](https://thunderstore.io/package/Rune580/Risk_Of_Options/) in the r2modman profile (it is only referenced for the settings menu). `dotnet build -c Release` builds the plugin and copies it into the r2modman Default profile; add `-p:CopyToProfile=false` to skip the copy. `./build.sh` also writes the Thunderstore zip to `dist/`. The default game and profile paths are in `Directory.Build.props`; override them on the command line or in a `Directory.Build.props.user` file.

`tests/` holds in-game tests, run with [ModTestRunner](https://github.com/jacob5567/ModTestRunner). ModTestRunner builds them into a separate dev-only plugin, which is never packaged: clone it and run `./build-tests.sh <path to this repo>` there. Then start a run with DebugToolkit installed, and enter `mod_test --mod SevenUI` in the console, which runs every check and takes every screenshot. `mod_test --mod SevenUI --suite screenshots` runs only the screenshots: it sets up each restyled part of the HUD (items, the scoreboard, shield and barrier, cooldowns, the N'tormat's pages beside the key item, the interaction prompt, a Lunar Pod's prompt, the tutorial popups, a pickup notification, the Winnowing's trial objectives, ally cards, damage numbers, damage numbers from Lost Seer's Lenses' instakills, the boss bar, and the HUD of each survivor with elements of its own: Railgunner, Void Fiend, Seeker, Operator, Drifter and Acolyte) and saves a screenshot of each in SevenUI's look and the game's, in `BepInEx/ModTestRunner/Screenshots/`. The screenshots turn the player into each survivor and back, and start the teleporter, last in their suite; each suite starts on a fresh stage as the survivor the run began with. `mod_test --mod SevenUI --suite scoreboard` runs the scoreboard's checks and its screenshots, each on two boards: as rows, with you alone, and as tabs, crowded with three stand-in players with 100 items each, so its rows collapse. The stand-ins are Commandos on your team with a player's controller, since neither the game nor DebugToolkit can add another player during a run (DebugToolkit's `spawn_ai` allies aren't players, so the scoreboard doesn't list them). On each board it checks, with a spread of items and with 3 to 100 different items, the items' grid, the board's place with `ScoreboardBesideRunInfo` on and off, the docked panel describing the item, equipment and key item pointed at, and the item bar's tooltip over the open scoreboard. It also checks the inspect panel (`--suite scoreboard_inspect`): shown through the game's controller with Alien Head and with a small chest's own info, its width, gap, face, strip, icon, fonts and colors, and, taken from the game's panel before the mod touched it, that turning `FlatPanels` off puts every value back, removes the strip, and on again styles it. With LookingGlass installed it also checks, with text as LookingGlass writes it for an item with stats, the stats column (the line, the column's width and place, a row per stat, the colors, fonts and right edges, and the description without the stats), that a chest or the setting off shows no column, and that `FlatPanels` off puts the game's text, gap and objects back; these skip without LookingGlass. It also checks that three stand-ins with 10 items each keep their rows at full size, that pointing at another player's row shows their items, and that with the scoreboard open the rows collapse as the stand-ins pick up 90 more items each, come back as rows when they drop them, and collapse again, then turns the flat look off. `--suite scoreboard_rows` and `--suite scoreboard_tabs` run one board's checks and screenshots, with the collapse while open in each. `mod_test --mod SevenUI --suite boss_bar` makes a boss group of Lemurians with their AI off and checks the boss bar's parts against what the bodies hold: none for a boss with only health, then the shield, the void shield (Plasma Shrimp), the barrier, a curse and the execute line of a frozen boss (and none when it is immune to executes), each in its place and color, the health and shields ending where the game's fill does, the trail after a hit to its health and none after a hit to its shield or barrier, the healed part, Lunar Ruin, Old Guillotine's line on an elite boss, two bosses' parts summed, and the parts gone and back with the flat panels off and on; it shoots the shield, barrier, curse, execute line and the two bosses in both looks. `mod_test --mod SevenUI --suite pickers` opens every item picker in the game (Command for each group of items and equipment, and for an item it offers alone, and every other picker panel, opened from a Command cube), checks its layout and screenshots it in both looks, and checks the descriptions with LookingGlass's setting off and on. `mod_test --mod SevenUI --suite countdowns` spawns each countdown panel from the game's prefab (the doom timer's and the trial's are skipped if the game's catalog doesn't have them), checks it and screenshots it with 2:05 left, 5 seconds left and the flat look off.
